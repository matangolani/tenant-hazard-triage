Tenant Hazard Triage

Small open language models that sort social-housing repair reports by legal urgency

Since October 2025, Awaab's Law has required social landlords in England to make emergency hazards safe within 24 hours of hearing about them. Those reports arrive as informal tenant messages in a busy inbox, and the failure that matters is an emergency left unread.

I built a model that reads each message, decides whether it is an emergency, a significant hazard or routine, works out the legal deadline and flags anything urgent for a person. Commercial repair systems exist; the question here was whether a small open model running locally, so tenants' health details never leave the landlord, can do this reliably, and how you would prove it.

1Try it

Pick a test message to see what the system answered when it was scored, mistakes included.

2How it works

With no real tenant messages available, I turned the government's triage guidance into 38 rules, had a larger model write training messages in near-identical pairs that differ in one decisive detail, and kept only those an independent re-label agreed with. A 4-billion-parameter model was fine-tuned on them, then paired with a second small model that can escalate, but never downgrade, its answer.

3Results

Scored on two test sets frozen before training: 152 tenant-style messages and the 37 worked examples in the government guidance. The headline metric is emergency recall, the share of true emergencies caught; a silent miss is an emergency that was missed and not flagged for a person.

4Limitations

  • No real tenant text, and small test sets. The messages were written by a language model or are landlord summaries, with 71 emergencies in total, so intervals are wide.
  • Decision support, not automation. The system flags; a person decides.

5Run it

The repository has the rules, data generator, training notebook, evaluation, tests and a CI job that re-scores every committed result on each push.

ollama pull qwen3.5:4b && bash scripts/deploy_ollama.sh <gguf folder>
uvicorn api:app --app-dir src --port 8000      # demo at localhost:8000
